Name: Tonkotsu Ramen
Cuisine: Japanese
Time: 60 minutes
Difficulty: Hard
Dietary: dairy-free
Ingredients:
- 1kg pork bones (neck and trotters)
- 400g fresh ramen noodles
- 4 soft-boiled eggs, halved (marinated in soy sauce if desired)
- 4 slices chashu pork belly (store-bought or homemade)
- 2 sheets nori, halved
- 100g corn kernels
- 4 spring onions, sliced
- 2 tablespoons soy sauce
- 1 tablespoon mirin
- 1 teaspoon sesame oil
- 100g bean sprouts
Instructions:
1. Blanch pork bones in boiling water for 5 minutes. Drain and rinse thoroughly under cold water.
2. In a large pot, cover bones with 3 litres of fresh water. Bring to a vigorous boil and maintain a rolling boil for 45-60 minutes. The aggressive boil emulsifies the collagen into the broth, creating the milky-white tonkotsu colour.
3. Strain broth, season with soy sauce, mirin, and sesame oil.
4. Cook ramen noodles per package instructions. Drain.
5. Divide noodles between four large bowls.
6. Ladle hot broth over noodles.
7. Top each bowl with: two egg halves, chashu slices, nori, corn, bean sprouts, and spring onions.
Notes: Tonkotsu broth requires a vigorous boil — the cloudiness comes from emulsified collagen and fat, not a mistake. This is the longest recipe in the Japanese sub-collection.
